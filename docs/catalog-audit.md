# Catalog editorial audit
38 authored styles. Minimum lengths refer to the top and are coarse buckets, not measurements. Texture fit is editorial suitability, never an identity or ethnicity restriction. Close cuts remain available across all textures.

Buzz is very close and uniform; butch is a fuller uniform bristle; crew graduates toward a longer front; high and tight has a small top panel and a high close blend. Caesar has a compact blunt fringe; French crop has a fuller top and defined fringe; textured crop has broken ends. Ivy League retains enough short front for a part; classic side part has more overall length; comb over is a longer cross-top sweep. Shag has connected layers; wolf cut emphasizes a shorter full crown; mullet emphasizes a longer back. Rounded Afro keeps side volume; tapered Afro narrows toward the edges; short Afro is compact. Curly high top emphasizes height; curly taper preserves a rounded top. Mohawk is a continuous strip, faux hawk a blended ridge, frohawk a natural curl/coil strip.

Before public release, a barber should review all texture compatibility values, coarse minimum lengths, flat-top adaptability, bowl perimeter terminology, and side-treatment compatibility. Review generated illustrations individually against these distinctions. Waves require brushing/styling; twists require a separate styling service. Neither is produced by a clipper cut alone. No growth timelines, guard numbers, or straightening instructions are prescribed.
